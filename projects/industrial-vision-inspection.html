Problem

A soldered PCB can be wrong in a few ways. A part can sit in the wrong position, the wrong part can be fitted, or a solder joint can be bad. Some of these are easy for a camera to see. Others depend on small details, like the color bands that give a resistor its value.

How the board is imaged matters as much as the model. Solder and metal reflect light, taller parts cast shadows, and a board that sits slightly off position looks different to the camera.

My supervisor assigned work project by project. The main one was a PCB inspection demo for Automation Taipei 2024, an industrial automation trade show. The other was keeping the company’s showroom demo working well.

My role and contributions

There were two pieces of work. The expo project was mine from setup to demonstration. In the showroom demo, my job was maintenance, testing, and retraining. Both were on the application side of Solomon’s vision platform.

Expo project (mine)

  • Set up a fixed camera and lighting rig for imaging soldered PCBs.
  • Collected and labeled about 200 PCB images.
  • Trained and tuned the inspection models in Solomon’s vision platform. They checked component placement, wrong components (for example, a resistor’s value from its color bands), and solder condition.
  • Tested the models against reflection, shadow, and changes in board position, and reported what failed to the AI engineering team.
  • Demonstrated the system at Automation Taipei 2024.

Showroom demo

  • Maintained and tested the inspection software used in the showroom’s live demo.
  • Retrained the whole model to improve the live results.

Worked within Solomon’s vision platform, turning its detection tools into a working PCB inspection system.

Validation

I tested the expo models by changing what the camera sees, then reported what failed to the AI engineering team.

Reflection

Solder and metal surfaces reflect the fixed lights. I looked for cases where glare changed the result.

Shadow

Taller components cast shadows on the parts next to them. I checked how those shadows affected the checks.

Position

I moved the board around in the frame to see whether each check still found the right part.

The expo project went from camera setup to a working demonstration at Automation Taipei 2024, and my test findings went to the AI engineering team. In the showroom, I retrained the live demo’s model and kept the demo running.

I presented the demo to visitors at the expo, and Solomon’s chairman showcased it to Taiwan’s Vice President.
